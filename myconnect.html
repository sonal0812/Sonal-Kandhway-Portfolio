Building a central platform for a company that had no central platform.

Thousands of employees with no shared space for announcements, career opportunities, or colleague recognition. Internal job postings were missed. Achievements went unacknowledged. The communication gap wasn't just an inconvenience — it was silently eroding culture.

01 — Problem Statement

No feed. No jobs board.
No recognition.

The company lacked a dedicated platform for effective employee interaction and communication. Without a centralised post feed, key messages went unnoticed. Without a visible jobs board, internal career opportunities were missed. Without recognition features, achievements and milestones went uncelebrated — and employees felt disconnected from the company's culture and goals.

01
Career Opportunities Missed

Employees were unaware of internal job openings. No simple or consistent referral process existed. Valuable talent was being lost to external hiring when internal candidates existed.

02
No Central Communication Hub

Management announcements, promotions, and company news had no consistent channel. Critical updates often reached only part of the organisation — or arrived too late to be useful.

03
Recognition Gap

Achievements, promotions, and milestones went unrecognised. Employees had no visible, engaging space to celebrate each other — weakening team cohesion and morale across the organisation.

02 — Discovery

5 questions to frame
the research.

Who?

Target audience spans the full org hierarchy — CEO to intern. Age group 20–60. Different information needs, different digital comfort levels, one platform to serve all.

What?

What frustrates users about the current intranet? What features do they actually want? What's the first thing they'd look for when opening the app?

When?

When during the workday do employees need the platform most? When do they feel most disconnected from company news and updates?

Where?

Where in the current intranet do users get confused or drop off? Where are the gaps between what's communicated and what's received?

Why?

Why do people use (or avoid) the current portal? Why are certain features underused? Why is employee engagement lower than it should be?

Secondary Research: Reviewed Facebook Workplace and Zoho Intranet to understand established patterns before starting requirements gathering with stakeholders.
03 — Primary Research

6 users. 2 age groups.
One clear picture.

Ran a screening survey across social media groups to identify the right participants, then conducted in-depth interviews with 6 people — 4 aged 18–30 and 2 aged 30–65. The age split was deliberate: different digital behaviours, different communication expectations.

Interview Insight 1

"It would be great if the platform had a user-friendly interface accessible on both mobile and desktop." — Accessibility across devices was a universal expectation, not a nice-to-have.

Interview Insight 2

"Sometimes I miss out on career growth opportunities because I'm not aware of internal job vacancies." — Career visibility was one of the highest-pain unmet needs identified.

Interview Insight 3

"I often feel disconnected from the company when I'm uninformed about crucial news and events." — The emotional impact of communication gaps was more significant than expected.

Interview Insight 4

"It would be great to have a dedicated space for recognition and appreciation." — Celebration features were explicitly requested — not just by junior staff, but across all seniority levels.

07 — Testing & V2 Iteration

V1 feedback.
V2 response.

I shared the Figma prototype with 2–3 team members before development began. Their feedback was direct — and shaped the entire second version.

V1 Feedback received: "The UI looks too simple and needs more visual engagement." · "Incorporate AI-powered solutions." · "Display team hierarchy and achievements prominently on the dashboard." · "Convert the Feed section into the main dashboard." · "Include Quick Action options."
V2 Change 01
Feed became the main dashboard

The original design had a separate dashboard and feed. After feedback, the post feed was elevated to the primary landing view — with team activity, announcements, and updates all visible immediately on login.

Outcome: Users landed where the content was. No navigating to find what's happening.
V2 Change 02
Quick Actions added throughout

High-frequency tasks — post creation, job referral, viewing org chart — were buried in navigation. V2 introduced persistent Quick Action shortcuts surfaced at every key touchpoint.

Outcome: Task completion speed improved for the most common daily actions.
V2 Change 03
AI-assisted post creation

Employees wanted to share thoughts and work knowledge but found writing posts time-consuming. V2 added AI-assisted post creation — users could share rough ideas and have the AI help structure and refine their content.

Outcome: Post frequency increased. Employees who previously never posted began sharing regularly.

08 — What I Learned

Five things I carry
into every project.

🔄
Always gather early feedback

The V1 → V2 iteration proved this definitively. Getting 3 users to review a prototype before development saved weeks of rework. Feedback early costs hours. Feedback late costs sprints.

🏠
Dashboard design is high-stakes

The landing experience determines whether users return. A clear, engaging dashboard with relevant content and quick access to frequent tasks is the difference between adoption and abandonment.

🤖
AI features need user context

AI-powered post creation only worked because we understood why users weren't posting in the first place. AI without user context is a feature. AI with user insight is a solution.

🏗️
Hierarchy needs to be visible

Employees don't just want to communicate — they want to understand the organisation they work within. Making the org chart accessible and visual was one of the highest-rated features in post-launch feedback.
