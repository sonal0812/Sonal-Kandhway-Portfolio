VarMiner
Cloud

Redesigning a complex genomics platform so clinicians spend less time navigating and more time diagnosing.

Genome analysts were working with a monolithic legacy interface that gave every genetic finding equal visual weight — critical variants alongside routine ones. For clinicians making life-altering diagnostic decisions, this wasn't a UX inconvenience. It was a clinical risk.

01 — Problem Statement

When the interface slows down
a diagnosis.

Genome analysts and clinicians need a user-friendly, scalable, and intuitive platform to efficiently analyse genetic data and generate clinical reports — because the current legacy system is difficult to navigate, time-consuming to use, and not designed for modern SaaS delivery, making it challenging to onboard new clients and scale operations.

01
Complex Multi-Step Workflows

Variant analysis workflows — germline, trio, somatic — each required multiple steps with no clear progress indication. Users frequently lost their place mid-workflow.

02
Data Overload

The interface displayed maximum data density with minimal hierarchy. Critical findings were visually indistinguishable from routine results. Cognitive load was high; confidence was low.

03
Steep Learning Curve

New users needed significant training before becoming independently functional. Competitive platforms were being adopted by clients because VarMiner's interface was too complex to onboard at scale.

02 — Discovery

5 questions before
any wireframes.

Before research, I used the 5Ws framework to structure what I needed to learn — and from whom.

Who?

Primary users: Genome Analysts, Clinical Geneticists, Genetic Counselors, Lab Technicians. Decision-makers: product owners and clinical leads who determine platform adoption.

What?

What tasks do users perform? What functionalities are non-intuitive? What do users expect from a modern genomics interpretation tool?

When?

When do major pain points occur during the diagnostic workflow? When are real-time reporting or support needed? When was the last meaningful UX revision?

Where?

Used in clinical labs and hospital settings. Integration points with external clinical databases. Where do users drop off or create workarounds?

Why?

Why are certain features underused? Why do users feel unconfident in the platform's suggestions? Why is onboarding failing for new clients?

Key insight: Every competitor had strong variant annotation and AI prioritisation. The UX gap was consistent — complex navigation with steep learning curves and poor data readability. That was VarMiner's opportunity.

User Interviews

Interviewed 2 clinical users via video call (users were based in Bangalore). I observed their behaviour while they answered and noted every hesitation.

"I observed the users' behaviour while they answered our questions. My team member noted all responses during the session."
Finding 1

Clinicians and genome analysts struggle with a complex, non-intuitive interface. First-time users face a steep learning curve with no guidance.

Finding 2

Users feel overwhelmed by the volume and layout of genetic data. No clear way to prioritise which findings need immediate attention.

Finding 3

Lack of clarity in navigation and reporting workflow. Users lack confidence in the system due to unclear feedback and unpredictable tool behaviour.

06 — Design System

Tokens first.
Components second.

Before high-fidelity screens, I built the design system using Figma variables for tokens — colour, spacing, typography, shadow, and components.

📸 Add design system overview
assets/images/varminer-design-system.jpg · assets/images/varminer-tokens.jpg · assets/images/varminer-components.jpg

Token structure

  • Colour tokens: Clinical priority levels (critical, warning, routine) mapped to semantic colours
  • Typography scale: Optimised for data-dense clinical displays
  • Spacing grid: 4px base unit consistent across all screens
  • Shadow system: Elevation hierarchy for panel layering

Component library

  • Variant cards with urgency indicators
  • Data tables with sortable, filterable columns
  • Clinical report templates
  • Status chips: Pathogenic / VUS / Benign
  • Navigation patterns for multi-step analysis flows

08 — Testing & V2 Iteration

V1 wasn't wrong.
It just wasn't enough.

After V1, we conducted usability testing with small and large user groups. Three friction points emerged clearly.

Finding 01
High-frequency actions were buried

Users performing repetitive daily tasks — running analysis, generating reports — had to navigate multiple screens to reach them. The workflow wasn't wrong; the access pattern was.

V2 Fix: Introduced Quick Launch — a persistent shortcut panel for the 5 most frequently used actions, surfaced at every point in the workflow.
Finding 02
Add Sample flow had too many steps

The sample intake process required users to navigate through 4 separate screens to add a single sample. Lab technicians processing high volumes found this unacceptably slow.

V2 Fix: Redesigned Add Sample as a single progressive form — all steps visible in one panel, required fields surfaced first, optional metadata collapsed.
Finding 03
Variant Interpretation caused cognitive overload

The Variant Interpretation section displayed full annotation data for every variant simultaneously. Clinicians reported scanning the same data multiple times because nothing directed their attention.

V2 Fix: Rebuilt Variant Interpretation around clinical urgency. Critical variants surfaced at top with visual priority indicators. Secondary data collapsed behind expand controls.

09 — Outcome

Streamlined. Intuitive.
Scalable.

⚡
Quick Launch Adopted Immediately

The Quick Launch feature became the primary navigation pattern for experienced users within the first week of V2 deployment.

📉
Reduced Task Completion Time

Add Sample flow reduction from 4 screens to 1 progressive form. Variant Interpretation review time reduced through visual prioritisation.

🎨
Full Design System Delivered

Token-based design system in Figma with clinical-specific components. Consistent visual language across Patient, Sample, Variant, and Report screens.

📈
Scalable for New Clients

Updated interface designed for SaaS delivery — easier to demo, easier to onboard. The previous monolithic UI was a barrier to client adoption.
